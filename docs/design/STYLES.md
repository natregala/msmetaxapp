## 1. How to use this document

Use this file alongside the project's AGENTS.md, product context, financial-record contracts, and mobile/bilingual UI skill. It records the latest visual direction and separates user preferences from design proposals.

The user has now attached and selected the Buwis Folder character for refinement. Preserve its friendly folder, tax papers, expression, and green/yellow/cream/coral colors, while simplifying its painted appearance into a modern, editable SVG. The earlier NEGOSYO / Bayong Buddy board remains the reference for broad shapes, strong color planes, and playful character. Its shopping-bag subject is not part of the selected direction.

This latest attachment supersedes the earlier rejection of the folder board as a blanket exclusion of that subject. The selected character should be refined faithfully; the newly drawn SVG is a proposed execution for review, not automatic approval of every geometry, color token, or future brand application.

Open visual decisions do not block functional work already requested by the user. Use reversible, clearly documented provisional choices when necessary. Do not treat a recommendation here as final brand approval, or add a new approval step to routine implementation.

This document does not expand the supported tax workflow, authorize government filing, or make claims that the product has been validated with business owners.

## 3. Brand character and preferred visual reference

Aim for a tool that feels capable, welcoming, energetic, and familiar to its intended owners.

Use two references for distinct purposes:

- The latest attached Buwis Folder image defines the subject, character, composition, and color family to preserve.
- The earlier board headed "Manila business, more character", particularly its middle NEGOSYO / Bayong Buddy panel, informs the broad rounded geometry, lively expression, and strong color planes.

The requested refinement should look like the same character carefully redrawn for a real brand. "Higher detail" means deliberate curves, accurate symbols, clean joins, and reliable exports. It does not require more decoration.

| Element | Current direction |
| --- | --- |
| Main shape | A broad forest-green folder with a purposeful slight tilt and rounded corners |
| Defining color block | A substantial mango-yellow lower-right pocket/corner |
| Documents | One dominant cream form and one offset supporting sheet, angled above the upper right |
| Tab | A mango index tab at the upper left; outlined BUWIS wording in the larger mark |
| Face | Dark oval eyes with cream highlights, coral cheeks, asymmetric brows, and a restrained curved smile |
| Depth | A limited number of adjacent flat color planes |
| Outlines | Selective structural separators; avoid heavy or inconsistent borders around every object |
| Character stance | Two compact feet on the full mascot; omit them where a compact silhouette works better |
| Lettering | Clear, chunky tab lettering; no brush-script wordmark added to the mark |
| Composition | Generous space around one recognizable character |

Remove paper grain, painted gradients, fuzzy shading, irregular tracing, unnecessary form lines, decorative rays, and the ground shadow from the core logo. Keep intentional asymmetry: straightening every edge and expression would lose part of the selected character.

The tax connection comes from the folder, forms, BUWIS tab, and Philippine peso cue, supported by clear product wording. Filipino references can also come from Tagalog interface copy and the business context. Treat recognition and cultural familiarity as design hypotheses to evaluate with owners.

The barong-collar detail, storefront landmarks, shopping handle, and extra financial symbols are not part of this direction.

## 4. Selected subject: Buwis Folder

The user selected this subject for a cleaner vector refinement. BUWIS is a descriptive tax label on the folder tab; the product name remains undecided.

### Preserve

1. Broad forest-green folder, including a modest darker side plane.
2. Mango tab at the upper left and a large mango corner at the lower right.
3. Angled cream form with a clear peso symbol and a few substantial fields.
4. Warm expression, oval eyes, cream highlights, coral cheeks, and slightly asymmetric brows.
5. Compact feet in the full character, preserving its friendly stance.

### Simplify deliberately

- Draw the mark with editable paths and shapes. Do not embed a raster image in an SVG wrapper or use noisy automatic tracing.
- Use a consistent family of corner radii and smooth curves.
- Keep the face above the front seam, with clear space around the eyes and smile.
- Keep the yellow corner large enough to remain a defining feature when reduced.
- Make BUWIS and the peso symbol vector outlines so they do not depend on an installed font.
- Remove tab text, fine paperwork, and feet from the compact version as needed; preserve the same folder silhouette, face, and color blocking.
- Use transparent knockouts for one-color facial and paper details. Recoloring every shape to one fill is insufficient.
- Keep external fonts, embedded bitmaps, scripts, filter effects, and raster textures out of production SVGs.

A descriptor such as "Business records and tax preparation" can clarify the category during evaluation. It belongs outside the core mark and may be translated separately. The character does not signify government endorsement, a completed filing, or a verified calculation.

## 5. Vector asset system

Use the following file roles for this refinement. These are adaptations of one character, with a level of detail suited to their intended placement.

| Asset | Intended use | Intended size guidance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `buwis-folder-primary.svg` | Full mascot with tab wording, form details, and feet; onboarding, larger brand placements, presentations | Use at 192 CSS px wide or larger; smaller placements should use a simplified variant |
| `buwis-folder-compact.svg` | Simplified folder for headers and small brand placements; no feet or tab lettering, fewer form details | Use at 48 CSS px wide or larger; 64 px gives the face more room |
| `buwis-folder-app-icon.svg` | Compact character on a mango rounded square | Use at 48 px wide or larger; the tab has an ink outline to separate it from the mango tile |
| `buwis-folder-monochrome.svg` | One dark ink with transparent negative-space details; compact silhouette | Use at 48 px or larger on light, simple backgrounds; interior details are transparent cutouts |
| `buwis-folder-reversed.svg` | Light mark with transparent negative-space details | Use at 48 px or larger on dark, simple backgrounds |
| `buwis-folder-favicon.svg` | Minimal silhouette and facial cues | Use at 16, 24, or 32 px; prefer this adaptation over shrinking the detailed compact mark |
| PNG exports and preview | Raster use in slides, documents, and applications that do not support SVG | Choose an export large enough for the intended display; retain SVG as the editable source |
| ZIP package and README | Complete variants and usage notes | Keep the package together when handing it to the development team |

Raster exports were visually inspected at these canvas widths: primary 128/192/256 px; compact 32/48/64/96 px; app icon 48/64/128 px; monochrome 32/48/64 px; favicon 16/24/32 px. The tiny variant keeps a clearer face at 32 px than the detailed compact mark. The guidance above reflects those rendering checks; it is not real-device or owner-validation evidence. The reversed mark was also inspected on a dark background. Use the SVG at any larger size without enlarging a raster preview.

Clear space: reserve approximately one main eye's width outside the visible artwork on every side. Measure from the outermost paper, tab, foot, or folder edge, not from a padded SVG canvas. Give the app icon its own consistent internal padding.

Use the full-color mark on white or warm cream when possible. Use the appropriate one-color or reversed variant when the background would obscure the face or document edges. Avoid busy photographs behind the mark. Preserve aspect ratio, the relative paper/tab positions, the face, and the yellow corner. Do not add drop shadows or stretch individual parts.

The final product wordmark remains a separate design task once the name is chosen. Do not treat the descriptive tab wording as a required untranslated interface label.

## 6. Proposed vector palette

The user asked to preserve the selected image's green, yellow, cream, and coral appearance. The following normalized values define this proposed SVG redraw. They retain that color family; the exact values are design choices, not a claim that the user approved individual hex tokens or that every source pixel has been sampled.

| Color | Hex | Role in the mark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Forest | `#176347` | Main folder body |
| Ink | `#073E30` | Face, tab lettering, peso symbol, structural depth, and feet |
| Mango | `#FFC63D` | Tab, large lower-right corner, and app-icon background |
| Cream | `#FFF7E3` | Main document and eye highlights |
| Coral | `#F8836B` | Small cheek accents |
| Sage | `#98A98A` | Simplified form fields and quiet paper details |
| Paper shade | `#E8DFC4` | Offset supporting sheet and paper depth |

Forest, mango, cream, and coral carry the main identity. Ink, sage, and paper shade support definition. Keep the proportions purposeful: a large green body, clear mango accents, cream paperwork, and small coral details.

The SVG palette is not a complete UI token system. White surfaces and restrained neutral borders may still support forms, records, and document review. Earlier cobalt and violet alternatives are not part of this selected refinement.

Implementation guidance:

- Use expressive color primarily in the identity, illustrations, selected controls, and limited emphasis.
- Keep working surfaces quiet enough to compare amounts, inspect notebook images, and correct records.
- Test every actual text/background pairing. The palette table is not a blanket contrast certification.
- Target at least 4.5:1 for normal text; apply the WCAG large-text definition before using its 3:1 threshold. [S4]
- Give errors, warnings, review states, and synchronization states separate semantic tokens.
- Always pair status color with meaningful text or another non-color cue.
- Do not let brand yellow or coral acquire an inconsistent meaning from screen to screen.
- Provide visible keyboard focus; test focus against surrounding colors.

Suggested token categories: brand, text, background, surface, border, focus, action, success, warning, error, and information. Name tokens by role rather than by a particular page.

## 7. Typography

Separate expressive identity lettering from task typography. The current vector tab uses outlined BUWIS lettering, and the peso symbol is also outlined. These graphic details must render without external fonts. They do not select a product wordmark or UI typeface.

- Future product wordmark and occasional display headings: chunky, upright, rounded custom lettering or a deliberately chosen display face that follows the preferred NEGOSYO reference. Keep the actual product name open until selected.
- Forms, navigation, tables, descriptions, and amounts: a highly readable UI typeface.
- During functional prototyping, a system UI font is acceptable.
- Record the font source, license, supported characters, fallback stack, and loading behavior when a font is selected.
- Verify the peso sign, punctuation, numerals, and all actual English/Tagalog copy.
- Use tabular numerals for comparable monetary columns.
- Start body text around 16px with comfortable line spacing; adjust from real-device review.
- Do not use decorative lettering for amounts, tax labels, validation messages, or dense record lists.
- Test long translations and large amounts without clipping or losing meaning.

Local painted lettering remains contextual research. For this iteration, the preferred NEGOSYO lettering is the more specific visual reference; do not introduce a fine brush-script treatment simply because it has a local association. No particular downloadable font is installed, licensed, or selected by this document.

## 8. Layout and component behavior

### Phone

- Make the main task clear on each screen.
- Use a readable single-column form where it helps.
- Show the amount, date, record type, and relevant status clearly in record lists.
- Place the primary save/review action where the user can find it without obscuring fields.
- Account for the on-screen keyboard and device safe areas.
- Avoid page-wide horizontal scrolling. Where a genuine data table needs it, keep headers and navigation understandable.
- Keep primary touch controls comfortably sized. The project target is at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels where practical; this is an enhanced design target, not a claim that every WCAG AA target has that minimum. [S5]

### Laptop

- Use additional width to show a source document beside the editable fields when helpful.
- Keep reading widths manageable.
- Preserve the same action names, status meanings, and core workflow.

### Components

- Buttons: clear verbs, discernible disabled/loading behavior, and stable layout.
- Inputs: persistent labels, useful examples, nearby errors, and retained values.
- Cards: content grouping with modest borders and spacing; decoration must not compete with records.
- Navigation: concise destinations, visible current location, and predictable return behavior.
- Language control: readily visible English / Tagalog labels; do not use country flags as language buttons.
- Empty states: one useful explanation, one relevant action, and optional small illustration.

Exact radii, spacing, shadows, font sizes, and navigation arrangement remain implementation proposals until evaluated on representative screens.

## 9. Language and voice

Use short, respectful, practical wording. Explain the task in the owner's terms before introducing specialized terminology.

Draft copy examples, to review with intended users:

| English | Tagalog draft |
| --- | --- |
| Add record | Magdagdag ng tala |
| Review records | Suriin ang mga tala |
| Save record | I-save ang tala |
| Saved on this device | Naka-save sa device na ito |
| Needs review | Kailangang suriin |

Do not treat these draft translations as a complete approved glossary. Validate terminology such as sales, expenses, net change, profit, tax, and saved/synced in context.

Changing language must preserve unfinished amounts, form values, selected files, review progress, and errors. Display formatting may change without altering the underlying financial value.

The identity can be playful while errors remain specific and calm. Avoid jokes, blame, forced slang, or celebratory language around missing records or failed saves.

## 10. Illustration, mascot, and motion

- Show recognizable tasks: recording a transaction, photographing a notebook page, checking a summary.
- Use selected business-specific objects after the target segment is settled.
- Keep characters capable and respectful.
- Use the full selected folder mascot in onboarding or guidance, with the compact folder where space is limited. Neighborhood scenes remain an optional, unselected supporting direction.
- The Buwis Folder is the selected subject for refinement. Its placement in each screen is optional; it should not interrupt review or add steps.
- For tax-related illustrations, show gathering records, inspecting a form, or reviewing an amount. A happy expression does not establish that tax preparation is complete.
- Use short, purposeful transitions and respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Avoid looping motion near amounts or document-review controls.
- A decorative animation is not evidence that an upload, save, sync, or filing succeeded.

## 11. Financial workflow states

The visual system must distinguish:

| State | What the interface should make clear |
| --- | --- |
| Original document available | The owner can inspect the source. |
| Extracted proposal | OCR output still needs the specified review. |
| Needs review | The next action and unresolved fields are visible. |
| Saved on device | The record may not yet be accepted by the server. |
| Sync pending/failed | Show understandable status and a safe next action. |
| Accepted/synced | Describe the actual confirmed system state. |
| Summary or tax draft | Show its period, coverage, and draft status. |
| Filed or paid | Use only when there is actual supported evidence of that event. |

Do not collapse all these states into a green checkmark. Keep unknown amounts distinct from zero. Preserve access to source details and correction history where the workflow requires them.

This styling document does not determine accounting or tax rules; use the project's domain contracts.

## 12. Subscription presentation — proposed future behavior

The user proposed subscriptions as a sustainability model. No price, tier, payment provider, billing period, or launch commitment has been selected.

Recurring benefits to test include easier repeated notebook imports, ongoing records, understandable summaries, and supported preparation/export workflows. A subscription should have a clear owner benefit beyond funding site maintenance.

If pricing or billing UI is implemented:

- Explain the actual included capabilities and any usage limits in plain language.
- Show the amount charged and billing interval together.
- Clearly distinguish a monthly equivalent from an annual amount billed upfront.
- Explain renewal, cancellation, and the selected policy for access to existing records.
- Show OCR/AI limits before the user begins a chargeable action.
- Keep record-review and save outcomes understandable if a billing problem occurs.
- Do not label a feature free unless its relevant limits and conditions are clear.
- Use explicit demo labels if payments are simulated.

Validate a proposed price against recurring owner value and the costs of hosting, storage, OCR/AI usage, payment processing, and support. Competitor subscriptions are evidence that the model exists, not evidence that the chosen segment will pay.

## 13. Owner evaluation

Evaluate the brand alongside a clear product description, not as an isolated aesthetic poll.

Ask intended owners:

1. What would you expect this app to help you do?
2. Does it seem relevant to your business?
3. Does it feel dependable enough for your business records?
4. Which part feels familiar, confusing, or too playful?
5. Can you identify it again at phone-icon size?

For this revision, pay particular attention to whether the owner expects tax/recordkeeping help, a shopping service, a school app, or a generic calculator. Ask the open-ended questions before offering these categories. Check recognition both with and without the small BUWIS label.

Then observe an actual task: add a record, switch language, correct an extracted value, and distinguish saved-on-device from synced.

Record observations separately from hypotheses. Do not mark the identity user-validated after only an internal team vote.
